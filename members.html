Group leader · Associate professor

Antton Alberdi

I lead a group of biologists and data scientists studying how animal–microbiota interactions shape ecological and evolutionary processes.

Assistant professor

Ostaizka Aizpurua

My research explores how animal–microbiota–environment interactions affect behaviour, ecology and evolution.

Data analyst

Iñaki Odriozola

I study microbial distributions and diversity through statistical modelling, including host–microbe and microbe–microbe interactions.

Laboratory specialist

Nanna Gaun

I perform DNA extractions and library building for the Earth Hologenome Initiative and related vertebrate microbiome projects.

Automation specialist

Jonas Lauritsen

I implement and optimise laboratory protocols for automation, drawing on a love of microbiology, learning and DIY projects.

Postdoc

Isolde van Riemsdijk

I study evolutionary interactions among two toad species, their microbiomes and the chytrid fungus.

Postdoc

Amalia Bogri

I develop and implement methods for micro-scale metagenomics.

Postdoc

Garazi Martin Bideguren

My work explores lizard gut microbiomes and the interactions between microbial communities and their hosts.

Postdoc

Jorge Langa

I apply hologenomics to conservation, including microbiome pre-conditioning of captive-bred animals before release.

PhD student

Adam Marques

Based at the University of Porto, I study how anthropogenic habitats affect the diet, microbiome and genome of fire salamanders.

PhD student

Elsa Brenner

I characterise gut microbiome differences in newts and salamanders to investigate environmental influences on microbial diversity and function.

PhD student

Lucas Padilha

I explore how the gut microbiota mediates evolutionary dietary shifts in Mediterranean wall lizards.

PhD student

Joana Fernandes

I investigate how dry-stone walls preserve biodiversity and ecosystem services, focusing on trophic interactions through metabarcoding.

PhD student

Nia Toshkova

I study the effects of climate change on bat hibernation and the molecular changes associated with prolonged winter inactivity.

PhD student

Zoe Horisberger

I develop large-scale untargeted imaging approaches to resolve host–microbiota interactions in space.
